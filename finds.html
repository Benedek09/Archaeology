The gallery

Copper Fragments

These pieces are a collection of ornately decorated copper fragments of various size, which likely originate from the Victorian period. In this era, copper had many uses, such as cookware due to its antimicrobial properties.

Copper's reddish-brown hue also made it useful for decorative items in households, which is what these pieces were most probably used for.

Brooch

This is a beautiful brooch or badge, that features acorns in the design. The object looks as if it is iron - possibly cast - and is definitely metallic but worn.

This pin was likely worn on clothes and could date as far back as the roman or saxon period, as brooches were a popular item of self adornment then.

Musket Ball

This is a damaged musket ball. It is of a traditional spherical shape, used in smoothbore muskets from their emergence in the early 16th century all the way to the mid-19th century when muskets became obsolete.

However, later versions of the musket often used various other ammunition such as Minié balls that were of a conical shape - which is superior for aerodynamics - so this ball is likely from an older model.

Bullet Casings

These are spent bullet casings, most of which are from .303 bullets. The .303 British is a 7.7mm calibre bullet that was the standard for British and Commonwealth rifles from 1889 to the 1950s.

They were most likely used during the Second World War and brought back by soldiers returning to their families as they are in relatively good condition.

Cartwheel Penny

This coin, minted in 1797, features King George III. The name 'cartwheel penny' is because of the coin's large size and resemblance to a cartwheel. It was created alongside the cartwheel twopence.

Designed by German engraver Kuchler, they were minted by Matthew Boulton in his private Soho Mint in Birmingham. It is believed 40,000,000 of the 1797 penny were minted, so it is quite common.

George Vth Penny

Minted in 1918, this penny is in quite good condition. Looking at the Britannia on the front, it appears there is no initial underneath the shield, indicating this was a Royal Mint penny.

Because this coin was minted in the war, some examples of it were not from the Royal Mint, bearing a 'KN' under the shield for Kings Norton Mint or more rarely 'H' for Heaton Mint.

WWI Peach Medal

This unintentionally curved medallion is a WWI peace medal, a type of commemorative medal that was distributed to mark the end of WWI. They come in many shapes and forms as many people made them.

This particular medal is a 33mm piece from 1919, featuring a warrior presenting a sword to an angle on one side and the inscription 'To commemorate the victorious conclusion of the Great War' on the other.
